# Quadratic Funding Revisited: A Mechanism Study of Octant

While studying public funding mechanisms in the Web3 ecosystem, I conducted a structured analysis of Octant, a protocol that redesigns Quadratic Funding (QF) by embedding it into an ETH staking–based system.  
This report documents the mechanism design, user flow, and governance structure of Octant, with a focus on how it addresses known limitations of traditional QF models.

## 1. Research Background

Quadratic Funding (QF) is widely regarded as an effective mechanism for allocating funds to public goods by amplifying collective preferences rather than capital concentration.

However, in real-world deployments (e.g., Gitcoin Grants), QF has revealed several structural challenges:

- Dependence on external matching pools  
- Reliance on identity or reputation systems to mitigate Sybil attacks  
- High cognitive and operational costs for ordinary users  
- Centralized coordination in execution and governance  

Octant proposes a different approach:  
instead of treating QF as a standalone matching algorithm, it embeds QF into a protocol-level capital and incentive system based on ETH locking and yield redistribution.

The goal of this study is not to evaluate Octant’s success, but to understand how its mechanism works under realistic constraints.

---

## 2. System Overview and Core Design

### 2.1 From Donations to Yield Redistribution

In traditional QF systems:

- Users donate funds directly  
- A matching pool amplifies contributions  

In Octant:

- Users lock ETH into the protocol  
- Yield generated by the system becomes the allocatable resource  
- Users decide whether their yield goes to themselves or to public goods  

Key shift:

Public funding is no longer an additional expense, but a redistribution choice over already-generated value.

This reframes public goods funding from charity to protocol-governed allocation.

---

### 2.2 Participant Roles and Flow

Core participants:

- **ETH Lockers**: provide capital and gain allocation rights  
- **Public Projects**: candidates for funding rounds  
- **Governance Actors**: manage parameters and protocol evolution  

Simplified flow:

1. User locks ETH  
2. Protocol generates yield  
3. User allocates yield (self vs. public pool)  
4. Public pool is distributed via QF variant  
5. Governance adjusts parameters over time  

---

## 3. User Participation and Experience

### 3.1 Entry Barrier and Its Function

At first glance, Octant’s entry barrier is high:

- ETH ownership  
- ETH lock-up  

However, this barrier is functional rather than arbitrary:

- Provides a stable capital base  
- Increases the cost of Sybil-style manipulation  
- Converts users from one-time donors into long-term participants  

Thus, the system trades inclusiveness for commitment and stability.

---

### 3.2 Yield Allocation as a Decision Mechanism

A key innovation in Octant is giving users explicit control over their yield:

- Claim for personal benefit  
- Allocate to public goods  
- Split between the two  

This design:

- Reduces psychological friction (no “extra spending”)  
- Makes public funding a rational allocation choice  
- Strengthens the user’s sense of responsibility for outcomes  

---

### 3.3 UX Limitations

Despite structural improvements, UX challenges remain:

- Locking → yield → allocation is not intuitively obvious  
- QF math is still abstract to non-technical users  
- Long-term consequences of yield sacrifice are hard to estimate  

Octant improves incentives, but not all cognitive complexity disappears.

---

## 4. Quadratic Funding Inside Octant

### 4.1 QF as a Subsystem, Not the Core

In Octant, QF is used only after the public pool size is determined.

Implications:

- QF no longer attracts capital  
- QF only expresses preference  
- Attack surface is significantly reduced  

This sharply contrasts with traditional QF, where donation and matching are tightly coupled.

---

### 4.2 Constraints on Strategic Behavior

Octant limits manipulation through protocol design:

- Allocation power derives from locked ETH yield  
- Individual influence is capped by system rules  
- Public pool size depends on collective behavior  

As a result, single-actor dominance becomes economically inefficient.

---

### 4.3 Comparison with Traditional QF

| Dimension | Traditional QF | Octant |
|---|---|---|
| Funding source | Donations | Protocol yield |
| User role | Donor | System participant |
| Sybil defense | Identity-based | Cost-based |
| Core risk | Matching pool abuse | Capital concentration |

---

## 5. Security and Attack Resistance

### 5.1 Protocol-Level Defense

Instead of identity verification, Octant relies on economic constraints:

- Real capital lock-up  
- Delayed and uncertain rewards  
- No guaranteed arbitrage paths  

This makes large-scale manipulation economically irrational.

---

### 5.2 Incentive Asymmetry

For attackers:

- Costs are immediate and certain  
- Returns are delayed and probabilistic  
- Outcomes depend on collective behavior  

Such asymmetry discourages rational attackers without requiring heavy surveillance.

---

## 6. Governance and System Evolution

### 6.1 Governance Scope

Governance in Octant focuses on:

- Parameter tuning  
- Rule updates  
- Protocol upgrades  

It does not directly intervene in funding outcomes, preserving mechanism neutrality.

---

### 6.2 Centralization Risks

Potential risks include:

- Early-stage parameter concentration  
- Governance complexity exceeding average user understanding  

These risks grow with protocol scale and must be actively managed.

---

## 7. Interpretation and Takeaways

### 7.1 What Octant Solves

- Reduces dependence on external matching funds  
- Embeds public funding into sustainable yield flows  
- Shifts Sybil defense from identity to economics  

---

### 7.2 Remaining Limitations

- Capital-based participation remains exclusionary  
- UX and education challenges persist  
- Long-term participation incentives are still unproven  

---

### 7.3 Broader Implications

Octant demonstrates that Quadratic Funding does not need to be a standalone algorithm.  
It can be a controlled component inside a broader institutional design.

---

## 8. Conclusion

Octant does not attempt to find a “perfect” Quadratic Funding formula.  
Instead, it constrains QF within a protocol that aligns capital, incentives, and governance.

This suggests a broader lesson for Web3 public goods:

> The effectiveness of a mechanism depends less on its math,  
> and more on the system it is embedded in.
